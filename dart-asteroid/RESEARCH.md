# Research: DART, Dimorphos and Hera

| Claim in the video | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 14,000 mph | DART hit at ~6.1 km/s (~14,000 mph) on 26 Sep 2022 | NASA DART mission page; Daly et al., Nature 2023 |
| Tiny moon circling a bigger asteroid | Dimorphos (~151 m, ~500 ft) orbits Didymos (~780 m, ~2,560 ft) | Daly et al., Nature 2023 |
| Orbit cut by 33 minutes | 11 h 55 min → 11 h 22 min (−33 ± 1 min) | Thomas et al., Nature 2023 |
| Tail 6,000 miles long | Ejecta tail > 10,000 km observed by Hubble/SOAR | Li et al., Nature 2023 |
| Hera just arrived | ESA's Hera reached the Didymos system in Nov 2026; first close-up images expected late Nov 2026 | ESA Hera mission pages / news, Nov 2026 |
| No crater, whole moon changed shape | Simulations matching the ejecta show DART likely caused global reshaping of the weak rubble pile rather than a small crater | Raducan et al., Nature Astronomy 2024 |
| Like a sandcastle | Dimorphos is a loosely bound rubble pile with almost no cohesion | Raducan et al. 2024; Barnouin et al., Nat. Commun. 2024 |

Wording note: the video says "may be no crater" because Hera's images are what will confirm it.
