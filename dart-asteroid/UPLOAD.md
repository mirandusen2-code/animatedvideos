## Description
On September 26, 2022, NASA's DART spacecraft hit Dimorphos, a 500-foot moon of the asteroid Didymos, at about 14,000 mph. It was humanity's first planetary-defense test, and it worked: Dimorphos' orbit got 33 minutes shorter (11 h 55 min → 11 h 22 min), and the impact threw out a tail of debris more than 6,000 miles long. Now ESA's Hera probe has arrived to inspect the damage, with its first close-up images expected this month. The twist: simulations suggest DART didn't leave a normal crater at all. Dimorphos is a loose rubble pile, so the impact may have reshaped the whole moon, like a sandcastle hit by a wave.

Sources: NASA DART mission results (Thomas et al., Nature 2023; Li et al., Nature 2023); Raducan et al., "Global resurfacing of asteroid Dimorphos by the DART impact", Nature Astronomy (2024); ESA Hera mission pages.
