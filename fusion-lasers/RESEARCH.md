# Research: laser fusion at the National Ignition Facility (NIF)

| Claim in the video | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 192 lasers | NIF focuses 192 laser beams on one target | LLNL, lasers.llnl.gov ("How NIF works") |
| A fuel pellet | A ~2 mm (0.08 in) capsule of frozen deuterium–tritium fuel, held in a gold hohlraum ~1 cm (0.4 in) long | LLNL NIF target pages; lmf.llnl.gov FAQs |
| Why: build a star on Earth | The goal of inertial fusion energy is a power source that works like the Sun: fusing hydrogen into helium | DOE / NNSA fusion pages; Physics Today, "Harnessing energy from laser fusion" |
| Fusion powers the Sun | The Sun fuses hydrogen into helium in its core | NASA / standard astrophysics |
| No carbon | Fusion emits no CO2 while running; its product is helium | DOE fusion energy FAQs |
| Fuel comes from seawater | Deuterium is extracted from ordinary water (≈1 in 6,400 hydrogen atoms). Tritium is bred from lithium in a power plant | DOE / ITER fuel pages |
| Smaller than a peppercorn | Capsule ~2 mm vs a peppercorn ~4–5 mm | LLNL; common size |
| Core hotter than the center of the Sun | NIF hot spots exceed 100 million °F; the Sun's core is ~27 million °F (15 million °C) | LLNL; NASA |
| Last year, one shot gave back 4× the laser energy | 7 Apr 2025: 8.6 MJ fusion yield from ~2.08 MJ laser energy (gain ≈ 4.13), NIF's record | LLNL news (llnl.gov/article/…); widely reported May 2025 |
| A real power plant is still years away | Gain is counted against laser energy on target; the lasers draw ~300 MJ from the wall per shot and fire a few times a day, while a plant needs ~10 shots per second | Physics Today; LLNL LIFE/IFE roadmap; DOE IFE program |

Wording notes
- "Last year" = 2025 (video made Nov 2026). First ignition was 5 Dec 2022 (3.15 MJ out from 2.05 MJ in).
- "Fuel comes from seawater" is true for deuterium; tritium comes from lithium. The short keeps the simple
  claim and the description says both.
