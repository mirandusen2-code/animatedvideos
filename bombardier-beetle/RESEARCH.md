# Research: the bombardier beetle

| Claim (script) | Check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Never eat this beetle." / "It shoots boiling hot spray out of its butt" | Bombardier beetles eject a hot, irritating chemical spray from the tip of the abdomen; the spray comes out as rapid pulses (~500 per second; 341–976 Hz measured with X-ray imaging) | Eisner & Aneshansley, *PNAS* 96 (1999); Dean et al., *Science* 248 (1990); Arndt et al., *Science* 348 (2015) |
| "Two chemicals mix inside it and explode" | Hydroquinones + hydrogen peroxide stored in a reservoir are released into a reaction chamber with enzymes (catalases, peroxidases); the reaction is explosive and produces the hot spray (benzoquinones). Simplified on screen as two glowing reservoirs feeding the chamber | Eisner & Aneshansley 1999; MIT News (2015) on Arndt et al. |
| "at two hundred twelve degrees Fahrenheit" | The spray is ejected at about 100 °C = 212 °F (the boiling point of water) | Eisner & Aneshansley 1999 |
| "Even if a toad swallows it, it keeps firing inside its stomach" | In lab trials with *Pheropsophus jessoensis* and Japanese common toads (*Bufo japonicus*), explosions were heard inside the toads after swallowing | Sugiura & Sato, *Biology Letters* 14 (2018); Science News; National Geographic |
| "until the toad throws it back up, alive" | In 43 % of trials the toad vomited the beetle (12–107 min after swallowing); all vomited beetles were alive and walked away (one escaped after 107 min) | Sugiura & Sato 2018 |
