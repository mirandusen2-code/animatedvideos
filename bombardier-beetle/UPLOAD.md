## Description
The bombardier beetle defends itself with a boiling hot spray: two chemicals mix in a chamber at the tip of its abdomen and explode, blasting out a pulsing jet at about 212 °F (100 °C), the boiling point of water. 🪲🔥
When a toad swallows one, the beetle keeps firing inside the toad's stomach. In lab experiments, toads threw up the beetle in 43% of cases, sometimes over an hour later, and every beetle that came back up walked away alive.

Sources: Eisner & Aneshansley, PNAS (1999); Arndt et al., Science (2015); Sugiura & Sato, Biology Letters (2018).
