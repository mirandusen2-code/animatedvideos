# Research: wombats poop cubes

| Claim in the video | Basis |
|---|---|
| "Wombats poop cubes" / "flat sides and sharp corners" | The bare-nosed wombat (*Vombatus ursinus*) is the only animal known to produce cube-shaped feces (Yang et al. 2021, *Soft Matter* 17:475–488). |
| "A wombat's gut has stiff spots and stretchy spots" | Two stiff and two more flexible regions around the circumference of the intestine (Yang et al. 2021). |
| "that squeeze the poop into corners as it dries" | Corners form in the last ~17 % of the intestine: faster contraction in the stiff regions, combined with drying of the feces in the distal colon (Yang et al. 2021; University of Tasmania / Georgia Tech press releases, Jan 2021). |
| "One wombat makes up to 100 cubes a night" | Researchers report 80–100 cubes per night (Yang & Hu, APS Division of Fluid Dynamics 2018 abstract; Georgia Tech). |
| "and leaves them on rocks to mark its turf" | Wombats are nocturnal and deposit feces on rocks, logs and other raised spots as scent markers (UTAS; Australian Museum). |
| "Round poop would just roll off" / "Scientists think that's why" | The leading hypothesis for the shape's benefit: cubes stay put on raised, sloping surfaces. Stated as a hypothesis ("scientists think"). |

Look (for the 3D): stocky barrel-shaped marsupial, ~3 ft long, grey-brown fur, short legs, small rounded ears, broad flat nose; cubes about 0.8 in (2 cm) across, dark brown.

Sources: Yang, Lee, Carver, Hu et al., *Soft Matter* 17 (2021) "Intestines of non-uniform stiffness mold the corners of wombat feces";
University of Tasmania news 28 Jan 2021; RSC news "Wombat research that's not to be sniffed at" (2021); phys.org Feb 2021.
