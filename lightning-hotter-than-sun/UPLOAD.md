## Description
A lightning bolt heats the air around it to about 50,000 °F, roughly five times hotter than the surface of the Sun (~10,000 °F). The air heats up so fast that it explodes outward, and that shock wave is the thunder you hear. The channel itself is only about an inch wide, and lightning flashes about 44 times every second somewhere on Earth.

Sources: National Weather Service ("How Hot Is Lightning?"); NOAA SciJinks; NOAA NSSL Severe Weather 101; NASA lightning sensors (Christian et al. 2003, JGR). Earth imagery: NASA Blue Marble (public domain).
