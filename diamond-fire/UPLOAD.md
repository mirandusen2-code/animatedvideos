## Description
A diamond is pure carbon, just like charcoal. Heat it to about 1,500 °F in pure oxygen and it burns, and every carbon atom leaves as carbon dioxide: the same gas you breathe out. Antoine Lavoisier showed this in 1772. And at room temperature graphite (pencil lead) is the more stable form of carbon, so every diamond is technically turning into graphite. It just takes far, far longer than a million years.

Sources: Lavoisier's diamond-burning experiments (1772); Live Science, "Can diamonds burn?"; thermodynamic data for carbon (graphite is more stable than diamond at room conditions).
