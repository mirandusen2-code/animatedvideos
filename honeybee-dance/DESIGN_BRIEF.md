# Design brief

A single worker is recognisable by its warm thoracic pile and dark banded abdomen. The wood-amber of the comb gives way to a cool field atlas. Gravity on the comb and the solar bearing on the map share the same screen axis. The diagonal straight run and the outward flight share a 45-degree bearing. Each spoken transition controls a visual change.

| Beat | Picture |
|---|---|
| Did you know a honeybee can give directions in the dark? | A close view of a live-motion reconstruction, with antennae, legs and abdomen in motion from frame zero. |
| Back in the hive… | A wider vertical comb; two nearby workers accompany the dancing forager. A fading trail reveals its alternating return loops. |
| Watch her abdomen… | A closer camera isolates a straight run and the side-to-side waggle. |
| An angle to the right… | An upward reference and rightward arc on the wax match to the sun bearing and flight heading outdoors. |
| The longer she waggles… | A sustained straight phase resolves into a longer route to a distant flower patch. |
| So if she runs straight up… | The example turns vertical. Hold the question and return to the opening framing. |

All bee poses and wax plates are native Cycles renders. A layered 2.5D composite reuses an articulated twelve-pose macro cycle without enlarging it. The field is a purpose-built explanatory map. The narrator is the licensed synthetic male Kokoro voice bm_george. Duration and captions follow the measured take. No borrowed wildlife footage or recorded animal audio is represented as a field observation.
