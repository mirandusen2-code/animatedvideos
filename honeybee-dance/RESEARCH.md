# How honeybees give directions in the dark

The subject is the recruitment dance of the western honeybee, *Apis mellifera*. The explanation concerns a forager dancing on a vertical comb inside the hive. Dancers perform a straight run while vibrating the abdomen, followed by alternating looping returns. The direction of the straight run relative to upward on the comb represents the direction of the food relative to the sun's horizontal bearing outside. A longer waggle phase communicates a greater distance. These relations are described in [NC State Extension's honeybee dance guide](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/honey-bee-dance-language) and [Dances as Windows into Insect Perception, PLOS Biology](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.0020216).

The rightward example is a teaching diagram with a 45-degree angle on both views. The vertical question therefore implies a flight toward the sun's compass bearing. The flight remains horizontal. Distances on the map, visible tracers and the animation speed are illustrative. The film gives no universal seconds-to-miles conversion, no exact distance to a particular real meadow and no implication that scent or other environmental cues are irrelevant. The illumination reveals what happens inside a normally dark hive. It is a 3D reconstruction.
