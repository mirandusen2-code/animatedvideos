# Research: the chicken gun (bird-strike testing)

| Claim in the video | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Engineers fire chickens at airplanes | Bird-strike tests fire bird carcasses (usually chickens) from compressed-air "chicken guns" at windshields, wings and engines | Wikipedia, "Chicken gun"; ASTM F330 (bird-impact test method) |
| At 350 miles per hour | At ~35 psi a 4-lb (1.8 kg) bird leaves the gun at about 350 mph (560 km/h) | Wikipedia, "Chicken gun" |
| Birds hit planes about 60 times a day, in the U.S. alone | FAA National Wildlife Strike Database: 22,372 wildlife strikes on U.S. civil aircraft in 2024, ≈ 61 per day (most are birds; reporting is voluntary, so the real number is higher) | FAA/USDA Wildlife Strike Report 1990–2024; AvWeb / General Aviation News, Aug 2025 |
| In 2009, geese shut down both engines of a jet over New York | US Airways Flight 1549, 15 Jan 2009: Canada geese knocked out both engines at ~2,800 ft after take-off from LaGuardia; the crew landed on the Hudson River and all 155 on board survived | NTSB report AAR-10/03 |
| The first windshields broke at just 75 mph | Early tests on windshields then used on U.S. airliners: a 4-lb bird punched through at only 75 mph (120 km/h) | Wikipedia, "Chicken gun" (history) |
| Every cockpit window must stop a four-pound bird | 14 CFR 25.775(b): windshield panes in front of the pilots must withstand, without penetration, a 4-lb bird at the design cruise speed | 14 CFR 25.775 (law.cornell.edu) |
| A real chicken, fired from a giant air cannon | Compressed-gas gun firing a chicken carcass (often in a foam sabot); e.g. Arnold AFB's gun had a 60-ft barrel | ASTM F330; DVIDS, "Chickens first took flight at Arnold AFB 50 years ago" |
| The chicken can't be frozen | The FAA guidance says test birds should not be frozen, since a frozen bird does not behave like a real one (the "frozen chicken through the windshield" story is a well-known legend) | Wikipedia, "Chicken gun"; Snopes, "Chicken gun" |

Wording notes
- "about sixty times a day" uses the 2024 figure (61/day); the video says "in the U.S. alone".
- No gore: the impact shows feathers and a white splat only.
