# Research: why airplane windows are round (the de Havilland Comet)

| Claim in the video | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Airplane windows used to be square, and the planes ripped apart in the sky | The Comet 1 (first jet airliner, 1952) had squarish windows; in 1954 two Comets broke up in flight (BOAC 781 off Elba, 10 Jan 1954, 35 killed; SAA 201 off Naples, 8 Apr 1954, 21 killed) | FAA Lessons Learned, "de Havilland DH-106 Comet 1"; Wikipedia "BOAC Flight 781", "South African Airways Flight 201" |
| Every flight, the cabin is pumped up like a balloon | The cabin is pressurized to an ~8,000 ft equivalent altitude while flying high; on the Comet 1 the difference was 8.25 psi, so the fuselage swells slightly and relaxes on every flight (one pressure cycle per flight) | RAE/Cranfield report (Atkinson et al.), "Behaviour of skin fatigue cracks at the corners of windows in a Comet I fuselage" |
| At a sharp corner, all that push piles up in one tiny spot | Squarish windows created stress concentrations much higher than anticipated at the corners | FAA Lessons Learned (Comet 1) |
| Flight after flight, a crack grows right there | Metal fatigue: cracks grew from rivet holes at window / escape-hatch corners | FAA Lessons Learned; Cranfield report |
| In 1954, two of the first jetliners broke apart in mid-air | BOAC 781 and SAA 201 (see above); the fleet was grounded | FAA; Wikipedia |
| Engineers sank one in a water tank and pumped it up, again and again | Comet G-ALYU was put in a water tank at Farnborough and pressure-cycled with water (water stores little energy, so a failure does not explode) | FAA Lessons Learned; Smithsonian, "Comet's Tale" |
| After about 3,000 flights, it split open at a window corner | G-ALYU had 1,230 real pressurized flights + 1,830 tank cycles (≈ 3,060) when the skin failed at the corner of a squarish forward escape-hatch window | FAA Lessons Learned |
| A round corner spreads the push all the way around | Later Comets (Comet 4) got rounded/oval windows and thicker skin; all airliners since use rounded window corners | FAA Lessons Learned; Wikipedia "de Havilland Comet" |

Wording notes
- The Comet windows were "squarish" (small corner radii), not perfect squares; the description says so.
- The fatigue cracks started at rivet holes near the corners (punch-riveting made it worse). The video simplifies to "the corner"; the description gives the full version.
- No victims shown: the break-ups are shown from outside, at a distance, with debris and mist only.
