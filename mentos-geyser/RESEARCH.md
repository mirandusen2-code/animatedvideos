# Research: Mentos + Diet Coke

| Claim in the video | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Drop Mentos into Diet Coke, and it blasts higher than a basketball hoop (10 ft) | A roll of Mentos dropped into a 2-liter bottle of Diet Coke typically makes a geyser of about 10–16 ft (NPR: "about 12 feet"; Steve Spangler: ~16 ft on slow-motion replay; amateur tests 9–15 ft for 5–20 Mentos); with a nozzle MythBusters reached ~29 ft | NPR (transcript 5341058); Steve Spangler Science; ABC News, "Science of Mentos-Diet Coke Explosions Explained"; Wikipedia, "Soda geyser" |
| Why? This bottle is secretly holding three bottles of gas | Colas are carbonated to about 3–4 "volumes": each liter of drink holds 3–4 liters of CO2 gas (at normal pressure). A 2-liter bottle holds ~6–7 liters of CO2, about three bottles' worth | Anton Paar wiki, "Carbon dioxide in beverages" (colas ~3.5–4 vol); UF/IFAS EDIS FS379; Penn State e-education (~0.5 % CO2 by weight) |
| The factory squeezed it in, and it's dying to get out | CO2 is dissolved under pressure at bottling; once opened, the drink is supersaturated and the gas wants to leave | Anton Paar wiki; Coffey (2008) |
| But a bubble can't start from nothing. It needs a rough spot | Forming a new bubble needs energy to create its surface (surface tension); bubbles form much more easily on nucleation sites: scratches, pores, rough particles | Coffey, "Diet Coke and Mentos: What is really behind this physical reaction?", Am. J. Phys. 76, 551 (2008); Penn State e-education |
| And up close, a Mentos is covered in tiny craters | SEM and AFM images show a rough, pitted candy surface (rms roughness ~440 nm) with many nucleation sites | Coffey (2008), SEM/AFM; C&EN Newscripts (2008) |
| Every crater spits out bubbles, all at once | The many sites nucleate CO2 bubbles simultaneously, releasing the gas very fast | Coffey (2008); khymos.org, "Soda fountain science explained" |
| And Mentos are heavy, so they sink, making bubbles all the way down | Mentos are dense and sink rapidly, so bubbles form throughout the depth of the bottle and the rising foam pushes the liquid out | Coffey (2008); Penn State e-education |
| And Diet works best: the sweetener makes bubbling even easier | Aspartame (and potassium benzoate) lower the surface tension of water, so bubbles form more easily; diet sodas make bigger geysers than regular | Coffey (2008); Mental Floss, "Why do Diet Coke and Mentos react?" |

Wording notes
- "Higher than a basketball hoop": the rim is 10 ft; the animated geyser peaks at ~13 ft, inside the reported 10–16 ft range for a full roll.
- "Three bottles of gas": 2 L × 3–3.5 volumes = 6–7 L of CO2 = 3–3.5 two-liter bottles.
- "Tiny craters": the SEM images show a pitted, porous surface; the craters in the animation are stylized and not to scale.
- "Heavy" = dense (sinks fast), said simply for a general audience.
- Roughness is not the only factor (Coffey: the gum arabic/gelatin coating and the sweetener matter too); the video names the two main ones. Rock salt and sand also trigger it, less strongly.
- Safety: the geyser is harmless but messy; the loop line "So stand back" plus the description suggest doing it outside.
