# Research: Prince Rupert's drop

| Claim in the video | Value used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The crack front races down the drop at ~1.5 km/s | "over 3,000 miles per hour" (1.45–1.9 km/s ≈ 3,240–4,250 mph) | Chandrasekar & Chaudhri (1994), *Phil. Mag. B* 70, high-speed photography of drop disintegration; Aben, Anton et al. |
| Made by dripping molten glass into cold water | yes | Standard production method; Royal Society demonstrations (1661, presented by Prince Rupert of the Rhine) |
| The outside cools first, the inside pulls it into compression | yes | Thermal tempering mechanism; Aben et al. (2016), *Applied Physics Letters* 109, 231903 |
| Surface compressive stress of up to ~50 tons per square inch | "50 tons per square inch" (~700 MPa) | Aben, Chaudhri, Purdue / Cambridge study (2017); compressive stress 400–700 MPa |
| The head survives a hammer blow | yes | Widely filmed; Chaudhri: the head withstands loads that would break ordinary glass, cracks cannot grow into the compressed skin |
| A snip of the tail makes the whole drop explode into powder | yes | Breaking the tail releases the tensile core; the crack bifurcates and the drop turns to powder |

Units: US (mph, tons per square inch). 700 MPa ≈ 101,500 psi ≈ 50.7 short tons per square inch.
