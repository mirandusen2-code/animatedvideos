# Research: the "immortal jellyfish" (Turritopsis dohrnii)

| Claim in the video | Basis |
|---|---|
| "This jellyfish can live forever" | Popular name "immortal jellyfish": it can reverse its life cycle and has no known age limit (biologically immortal). The loop line ("…unless something eats it") states the real limit honestly. |
| "When it gets old or hurt, it doesn't die" | When stressed, injured, sick or old, the medusa can revert to the polyp stage instead of dying (Piraino et al. 1996, *Biological Bulletin*; Wikipedia, AMNH). |
| "It melts into a tiny blob, sinks to the sea floor" | The bell and tentacles are reabsorbed into a cyst-like blob of tissue that settles on the bottom and forms stolons, then a polyp colony. |
| "and turns back into a baby" | The polyp is the juvenile (pre-adult) stage of its life cycle. |
| "Its old cells become brand new ones" | Transdifferentiation: differentiated adult cells turn into other cell types (Piraino 1996; Pascual-Torner et al. 2022, *PNAS*). |
| "grows up all over again" | The new polyp colony buds fresh medusae (genetically identical to the original). |
| "Again, and again, and again" | In the lab it has repeated the cycle multiple times (S. Kubota observed a *Turritopsis* rejuvenate 10+ times in two years); no known limit. |
| "unless something eats it" | In the wild most die from predation or disease as medusae (Wikipedia). |

Look (for the 3D): transparent bell about 4.5 mm across, bright red stomach and canals in the centre, up to ~90 white tentacles on the rim.

Sources: Piraino, Boero, Aeschbach & Schmid, *Biol. Bull.* 190 (1996); Pascual-Torner et al., *PNAS* 119 (2022);
AMNH "The Immortal Jellyfish"; The Scientist "An 'Immortal' Jellyfish Offers Clues into Biological Aging";
Wikipedia "Turritopsis dohrnii".
