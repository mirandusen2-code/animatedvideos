# Research: the tardigrade that survived space

| Claim (script) | Check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "survived ten days in open space" | Dried tardigrades flew on ESA's FOTON-M3 (Sept 2007) and were exposed to the vacuum of low Earth orbit (~270 km) for 10 days (the whole capsule mission lasted 12 days) | Jönsson et al., *Current Biology* 18 (2008); [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Research/Tiny_animals_survive_exposure_to_space) |
| "No suit. No air. Just vacuum." | Exposed directly to space vacuum (and cosmic radiation; some also to solar UV) | same |
| "barely half a millimeter long" | Most mature tardigrades are under 0.5 mm (range 0.3–1.2 mm) | Carolina Biological; UW–Stevens Point |
| "dries itself out, losing almost all its water" | Tun state: loses ~95–97 % of body water, metabolism nearly stops | UW–Stevens Point; Carolina Biological; McGill (up to 98 %) |
| "comes back to life when it gets wet again" | Rehydration revives the tun | same |
| "After the trip, some even had babies" | Some survivors reproduced after the flight | Jönsson et al. 2008 / ESA |
| "In 2019, thousands of them crash-landed on the Moon" | Beresheet (SpaceIL) crashed 11 Apr 2019 with several thousand dehydrated tardigrades (Arch Mission Foundation library) | Mental Floss, Big Think, Inverse |
| "And they might still be up there" | Survival is **unconfirmed**, so the script says "might" | same |
