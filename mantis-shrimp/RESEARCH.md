# Research: the mantis shrimp punch

| Claim (script) | Check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "can punch hard enough to break glass" | Large mantis shrimp are known to crack/smash aquarium glass (e.g. "Tyson", Great Yarmouth Sea Life Centre); aquarists use thick acrylic | Australian Geographic; aquarium literature |
| "Its club swings out at fifty miles per hour" | Peacock mantis shrimp (*Odontodactylus scyllarus*) club tip reaches up to 23 m/s = 51 mph | Patek, Korff & Caldwell, *Nature* 428 (2004); Patek Lab, Duke |
| "faster than you can blink" | Whole strike < 800 µs; a blink takes 100–400 ms | Patek Lab, Duke |
| "smashing snail shells in a single hit" | Peak forces ~1,500 N; they feed on snails by smashing the shells | Patek & Caldwell, *J. Exp. Biol.* 208 (2005) |
| "the water rips open into bubbles" | The strike is so fast that the low pressure creates vapour (cavitation) bubbles between club and prey | Patek et al. 2004 |
| "When they collapse, they hit a second time" | Two force peaks per strike: club impact + bubble collapse (cavitation force on average ~50 % of impact, up to 280 %) | Patek & Caldwell 2005 |
| "a flash of light and heat over seven thousand degrees Fahrenheit" | Collapse releases light (sonoluminescence), sound and heat; estimated > 4,000 °C (> 7,200 °F) for a tiny fraction of a second (estimate, not a direct measurement) | Patek & Caldwell 2005; Australian Geographic; ScienceBlog |
