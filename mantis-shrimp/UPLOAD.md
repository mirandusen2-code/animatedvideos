## Description
The peacock mantis shrimp swings its club at about 50 mph (23 m/s), one of the fastest strikes in the animal kingdom. Big ones have even cracked aquarium glass. 🦐
The club moves so fast that the water tears apart into tiny cavitation bubbles. When those bubbles collapse, they hit the prey a second time, with a tiny flash of light and heat estimated at over 7,000 °F for a split second.
So every punch hits twice.

Sources: Patek, Korff & Caldwell, Nature (2004); Patek & Caldwell, Journal of Experimental Biology (2005); Patek Lab, Duke University.
