# Research: the Moon is moving away from Earth

| Claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| "The Moon is running away from us." / "Every year, it drifts an inch and a half farther away." | Lunar laser ranging to the Apollo retroreflectors: the Moon recedes 3.8 cm (1.5 in) per year (NASA eclipse site "Measuring the Moon's Distance"; Wikipedia "Lunar laser ranging": 3.830 ± 0.008 cm/yr). Cause: tidal friction transfers Earth's spin to the Moon's orbit. |
| "That's about as fast as your fingernails grow." | NASA: 3.8 cm per year, about as fast as fingernails grow. |
| "When it was born, it was so close it filled the sky," | The Moon formed ~4.5 billion years ago much closer to Earth (a few tens of thousands of km vs 384,000 km today), so it looked many times larger in the sky. |
| "and a day on Earth lasted about six hours." | Estimates for Earth's rotation soon after the Moon formed are ~5–6 hours (Damn Interesting; standard tidal-evolution models). Said as "about". |
| "In 600 million years, it'll be too far away to cover the Sun." | Total solar eclipses end in ~600 million years (Eos "The End of the Eclipse"; Bend Bulletin); estimates range 600–620 million at today's rate. |
| Loop: "And it's still leaving. Right now," → "the Moon is running away from us." | Grammatical sentence across the seam. |
