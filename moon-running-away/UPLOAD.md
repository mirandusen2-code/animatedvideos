## Description
Lasers fired at the mirrors Apollo astronauts left on the Moon show that it drifts about 1.5 inches (3.8 cm) farther from Earth every year — about as fast as your fingernails grow. When the Moon was born it was so close it filled the sky, and a day on Earth lasted only about six hours. In roughly 600 million years it'll be too far away to fully cover the Sun, and total solar eclipses will be gone.

Sources: NASA "Measuring the Moon's Distance" (Apollo laser ranging); Lunar laser ranging (3.83 cm/yr); Eos "The End of the Eclipse". Earth imagery: NASA Blue Marble (public domain).
