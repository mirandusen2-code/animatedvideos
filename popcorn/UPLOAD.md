## Description
Every popcorn kernel holds a tiny drop of water inside a hard, watertight shell. Heat it and the water turns to steam, but the shell won't let it out: the pressure climbs to about 9 times normal air pressure. At around 356°F the shell cracks, the pressure drops at once, and the soft starch inside puffs into a foam about 40 times bigger than the kernel. Slow-motion footage shows the foam even kicks out a "leg" that pushes against the pan, so the kernel jumps and flips like a gymnast. And the pop you hear? It's the steam escaping, not the shell cracking.

Sources: Virot & Ponomarenko, "Popcorn: critical temperature, jump and sound", Journal of the Royal Society Interface (2015); Hoseney, Zeleznak & Abdelrahman, Journal of Cereal Science (1983) on pericarp pressure and expansion.
