# Research: two grapes in a microwave make plasma

| Claim in the video | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Put two grapes in a microwave, and they spit out a ball of plasma | Two grapes (or a grape cut almost in half) in a household microwave produce a bright plasma plume at the point where they touch | Khattak, Bianucci & Slepkov, "Linking plasma formation in grapes to microwave resonances of aqueous dimers", PNAS 116, 4000–4005 (2019); Physics World, "Grape plasma phenomenon explained at long last" |
| Why? Physicists want to squeeze light into tiny spots, and grapes do it | The Trent University team studied it because the effect, usually seen only in nanoscale metal structures, concentrates electromagnetic energy far below the wavelength; that could help focus light into tiny spots for imaging, spectroscopy and sensing (nanophotonics). Microwaves are light (electromagnetic waves) | Physics World; PBS NOVA, "How does microwaving grapes create plumes of plasma?" |
| A microwave is about five inches long | A 2.45 GHz oven wave has a wavelength of 12.2 cm = 4.8 in | Physics Today, "Fruit photonics and the shape of water" (12 cm) |
| But inside water, it shrinks to half an inch: exactly grape size | Water's refractive index at 2.45 GHz is about 9–10, so the wavelength inside is ~1.2–1.4 cm (≈ 0.5 in), about the size of a grape; that is why grape-sized water spheres act as resonators | PNAS paper; Science Friday interview with A. Slepkov (index ~10) |
| So each grape traps the waves, and they bounce around inside | Each grape acts as a resonant cavity for the microwaves; water's absorption helps the energy build up | PNAS abstract; Physics World ("the grape halves act as resonant cavities") |
| Where two grapes touch, it all piles into one spot the size of a pinhead | The two resonances couple into a hotspot at the contact point, about 1/100 of the 12 cm wavelength, i.e. ~1 mm (a pinhead is 1–2 mm) | Physics World; Physics Today |
| That rips electrons off the salt in the juice. Boom, plasma | The field at the hotspot is strong enough to ionize sodium and potassium in the fruit (emission spectra show Na and K) | PNAS paper; Physics World |
| They proved it with water beads: no skin, same fireball | Skinless hydrogel beads (>99 % water, briefly soaked in salt water) also make plasma when two touch; a single bead does not. So it is not the skin acting as an antenna, as was long assumed | PNAS paper; Physics Today; Boing Boing summary |

Wording notes
- "Microwaves are light": both are electromagnetic waves; the description says so in one sentence.
- "Half an inch": 12.2 cm / ~9 = 1.35 cm = 0.53 in; with an index of 10 it is 1.2 cm (0.48 in). "About half an inch" in the description.
- "Pinhead": the hotspot is ~1 mm; a common sewing pin head is 1–2 mm across.
- Safety: the loop line says "never do this at home" (it can damage the oven's magnetron and the plasma is very hot); the description repeats the warning.
